# Tada Words audio provenance

## Bundled spoken audio v1 — 2026-07-14

- Vendor/model: Cartesia Sonic 3.5 under the project's paid commercial plan.
- Teacher role: Katie leads 500 unique words with separate Read hint and Write
  prompt recordings, both at 0.67× with 120 ms protected tails. `bun` uses a manifest-documented Aurora
  override because two recognizers rejected Katie's isolated rendering.
- Brand/transition role: Aurora, one launch line, five rotating correct-answer
  micro-celebrations, one Quest-complete line, and one launch-generation source
  clip that is not selected as a transition.
- Delivery: 1,008 mono 44.1 kHz AAC-LC files, approximately 7.4 MB total.
- Runtime behavior: bundled and offline-first. No Cartesia key or runtime vendor
  request is shipped in the App. Apple en-US speech remains the fallback for a
  missing asset or a guardian-entered word outside the 500-word manifest.
- Reproduction: `Tools/Audio/generate_cartesia_offline_pack.sh` reads the two
  versioned manifests, limits Pro concurrency to three, retries transient API
  failures, and resumes without regenerating valid clips.

The generated voice output is not presented as a human performer or a celebrity
imitation. Musical World scores and functional non-verbal effects remain original
Tada Words synthesis code as documented below.

## Generation method

The app creates stereo 44.1 kHz PCM buffers at runtime from mathematical
oscillators, deterministic noise, musical event lists, and instrument-specific
amplitude envelopes. There are no bundled music files. Each original score is
24 seconds long and resolves to silence at the loop boundary so it can repeat
without a waveform discontinuity.

- Moonpetal Kingdom: an original eight-bar C-major score with harp arpeggios,
  a glockenspiel melody, a warm moving pad, and restrained sparkles. It is
  bright and dreamy while the repeated harmonic motion keeps practice moving.
- Build-It Bay: an original mechanical groove with soft kick, woodblock,
  bouncing bass, syncopated plucks, and a quiet chord bed. It has action and
  forward motion without using a loud drum kit.
- Paws & Pines: an original D-major marimba melody with alternating nature
  plucks, warm sustained harmony, gentle percussion, and three synthesized
  bird-like answers.

The score definitions live in `ProceduralAudioDesign.swift`; synthesis and DSP
live in `ProceduralAudioRenderer.swift`. Notes are rendered event-by-event
rather than scanning the whole score for every sample. Instrument timbres use
additive sine/triangle partials, stereo constant-power panning, and tailored
attack/decay shapes. Percussion uses synthesized pitch sweeps or deterministic
noise, so a build is reproducible.

Launch configuration applies the remembered world's voice/music preferences
without rendering its 8.5 MB score buffer. Score synthesis begins only when the
child lobby actually activates music, and it runs behind the actor boundary so
the main UI stays responsive. Re-activating the same already-playing world only
refreshes volume; it does not rebuild or restart the loop. The service keeps no
six-score cache. A two-entry LRU retains only the most recently used
world/rescue buffers (about 17 MB maximum), so Read recording recovery and app
foregrounding reuse PCM immediately while avoiding roughly 51 MB of all-world
persistent PCM memory. A render failure is never inserted and cannot evict a
healthy cached score.

Release-mode synthesis on the M4 development Mac rendered three complete
24-second stereo scores in 216 ms (about 72 ms per score) during the automated
test run on 2026-07-12. This is a development-host sanity check, not a claim
about device audio latency; supported iPhone and iPad hardware still needs the
final listening and fatigue pass.

When rescue time begins, the current world crossfades to a rhythm-layer variant
of the same composition. Both variants normalize to the same `0.40` PCM peak,
so urgency comes from rhythm rather than loudness. World changes use two music
players and a 720 ms linear crossfade. An async generation gate invalidates an
older fade if another world, rescue-state, recording, or app-state transition
supersedes it.

The launch mark is an original bright ascending gesture and sonic tail paired
with the bundled continuous Aurora “Ta-dá↗ woooords↘!” line. Correct, star, and
reward cues use
quicker upward movement and a little more harmonic lift, while valid retry
remains gentle and technical retry remains a neutral two-pulse cue. Functional
cues share stable meanings while their oscillator timbre follows the selected
world. Correct and Quest-complete transitions can add brief Aurora speech after
the immediate synthesized cue.

## Spoken-voice design

Canonical covered words use the bundled Katie voice, while brand and decorative
transition lines use Aurora. Both are pre-generated; no child text or usage data
is sent to Cartesia at runtime. The direction is bright, confident American
English with an energetic game-guide cadence and a warm teacher tone. It does
not imitate a celebrity or identify the output as a human performer.

Apple system text-to-speech is the resilient fallback. A deterministic policy
prefers an American-English female voice, choosing Premium or Enhanced quality
when installed. The current Mac development runtime exposes Samantha as its only
explicitly female `en-US` voice and reports it as standard quality. If a higher
quality voice is absent, Tada Words selects the best available female voice,
then a natural `en-US` voice, then Apple's language default.

The Aurora brand line lands over the original roughly 1.2-second sonic mark.
The generator trims the source components, keeps only the naturally level-to-
rising onset of the first syllable and makes that `/tə/` light and short. It
stretches one continuous `/dɑː/` source window through a rising pitch map and
never repeats a vowel slice. `words` falls across three
stages and connects through only an 8 ms click-safe crossfade. This makes the
prosody deterministic instead of asking TTS punctuation to infer the brand
contour.
Katie Read and Write prompts use 0.67×, with a 120 ms encoded tail after each
clip to keep final consonants clear. Words outside the versioned manifest pass
unchanged to Apple fallback synthesis at the same one-and-a-half-times-slower
cadence.

## Safety mix

Ambient scores normalize to a `0.40` PCM peak. Short cues are hard-limited below
`0.72`, then all audio passes through conservative node volumes (`0.105` music,
about `0.20` effects) and a `0.72` main mix. Word TTS ducks music to `0.018`.
Write practice keeps the score running quietly while the word is spoken and
restores its normal low level afterward. Read capture fades music over 100 ms,
stops both music players and effects, stops the engine, and deactivates the
audio session before the microphone starts. It fades the selected world score
back in only after recognition releases capture.

Correct, star, and reward cues use each world's tonic and major-scale tones so
they feel part of the active score. Retry cues remain shorter and lower in peak;
technical retry is intentionally neutral.

Automated tests cover score duration, stereo format, distinct score content,
activity across all four quarters, loop-boundary continuity, PCM peak limits,
rescue-layer peak parity, crossfade gain, TTS ducking, transition cancellation,
and the relative energy of celebration versus retry cues.

Simulator checks verify routing and state only. Speaker loudness, fatigue, and
the final mix must be approved on a real supported iPhone and iPad by listening
through two complete quests per world.
